# InvoiceBook — Product Requirements Document

**Owner:** Owais Ahmed
**Date:** 2026-09-21
**Status:** Approved for build (Phase 1)

---

## What we are building

A modern invoicing tool for a single business with a small team. The team creates invoices for customers, tracks whether each invoice is paid, and keeps proof of payment (bank transfer screenshots, receipts) attached to each payment. It supports monthly recurring billing so invoices that repeat every month get created on their own, and it keeps a full history so nothing gets lost.

Payments are tracked manually. The tool does not process cards. A staff member marks an invoice paid and uploads proof. The tool is built so online payment collection can be added later without a rewrite.

---

## Who uses it

- **Admin** — full access. Manages customers, invoices, recurring plans, team members, business settings, and can delete records.
- **Staff** — day to day work. Creates and edits invoices, records payments, uploads proof. Cannot manage team members or change business settings or hard delete.

Both belong to one business. There is no multi-tenant setup in Phase 1.

---

## The CTO's architecture decisions

We use Next.js App Router with TypeScript on Vercel. The database is Neon Postgres accessed through Drizzle ORM. Auth is Better Auth with email and password, sessions in the database, roles stored on the user. Attachments live in Vercel Blob as private files, served through short-lived signed URLs so proof of payment is never public. PDFs are generated on the server with React-PDF. Recurring invoices are generated by a Vercel Cron job that runs once a day. Money is stored as integer minor units (cents) with a currency code, never as floats. Every list query is indexed. Every write that touches more than one row runs in a transaction. Financial records are soft deleted, never hard deleted, so the audit trail survives. Email sending is deferred to Phase 2 but every invoice already carries the fields email will need.

# Phase 1 — Core loop

### User Story 1 => Admin Onboarding Flow

1. admin signs up with email and password and becomes the first user of the business with the admin role. admin lands on an empty dashboard that shows total outstanding, total paid this month, and overdue count, all zero for now. admin opens business settings and fills in business name, address, logo, default currency, and an invoice number prefix like INV. admin saves and these details will now appear on every invoice and every PDF. admin sees a short checklist that guides them to add their first customer and create their first invoice.

---

### User Story 2 => Staff Customer Flow

2. staff opens the customers page and sees a searchable list with name, email, outstanding balance, and last invoice date. staff clicks new customer and fills name, email, phone, billing address, and notes. email is required and must be a valid email. staff saves and the customer appears at the top of the list. staff can open any customer to see their full invoice history, total billed, total paid, and total outstanding in one view. staff can edit customer details anytime and old invoices keep the details they were created with.

---

### User Story 3 => Staff Invoice Create Flow

3. staff clicks new invoice and picks a customer. the invoice number is filled in for them using the business prefix and the next number in sequence, and they cannot type a duplicate. staff sets issue date and due date, due date defaults to thirty days after issue. staff adds line items, each with a description, quantity, unit price, and optional tax percent. the tool shows the running subtotal, tax total, and grand total as they type. staff can add a discount as a flat amount or a percent. staff can add notes and payment terms that show on the invoice. staff saves as draft or clicks finalize. a draft can be edited freely. once finalized the invoice gets locked for its core amounts, gets a status of unpaid, and can no longer have its number or totals changed, only payments recorded against it. staff can download the finalized invoice as a PDF that carries the business logo and details.

---

### User Story 4 => Staff Record Payment Flow

4. staff opens an unpaid or partially paid invoice and clicks record payment. staff enters the amount received, the date, and the method like bank transfer or cash, and uploads proof which can be an image or a PDF up to ten megabytes. the tool checks the file type and size and rejects anything else with a clear message. staff saves and the payment is added to the invoice, the outstanding balance drops by that amount, and the proof is stored privately. if the payment covers the full remaining balance the invoice moves to paid, if it covers part of it the invoice moves to partially paid. staff sees every payment listed on the invoice with its proof, and can click any proof to open it through a secure link that expires. staff can void a payment they entered by mistake and the balance and status recalculate.

---

### User Story 5 => Staff Invoice Tracking Flow

5. staff opens the invoices page and sees every invoice with number, customer, amount, amount paid, outstanding, status, and due date. staff can filter by status which is draft, unpaid, partially paid, paid, or overdue, and can search by customer or invoice number. an invoice that is past its due date and not fully paid shows as overdue with a red badge. staff can sort by due date or amount. staff clicks any invoice to open its full detail with line items, payment history, and proof. the dashboard totals at the top always reflect the current numbers.

---

### User Story 6 => Admin Recurring Plan Flow

6. admin opens recurring plans and clicks new plan. admin picks a customer, sets the line items just like an invoice, picks a start date, and sets the cycle to monthly. admin sets an optional end date or a number of cycles, otherwise the plan runs until paused. admin saves the plan as active. every day a scheduled job checks for plans that are due and creates the next invoice automatically as a finalized unpaid invoice, using the customer and line items from the plan and the correct new invoice number and dates. the plan records when it last ran so it never creates the same month twice. admin sees a list of plans with next run date, and can pause, resume, or edit a plan anytime. admin can also click generate now on a plan to create the next invoice by hand without waiting for the job, which covers the manual duplicate case.

# Phase 2 — Enhancements

### User Story 1 => Admin Team Flow

1. admin opens team settings and invites a team member by email and assigns them the staff or admin role. the invited person sets their password through the invite and joins the same business. admin can change a member's role or deactivate a member, and a deactivated member can no longer sign in. admin sees who created and last changed each invoice and payment.

---

### User Story 2 => Staff Invoice Email Flow

2. staff opens a finalized invoice and clicks send. the tool emails the customer the invoice with the PDF attached and a short message, using the business name as the sender name. the invoice records that it was sent and when. staff sees a sent badge on the invoice list. when a recurring invoice is generated the plan can be set to email it automatically on creation.

---

### User Story 3 => Admin Reporting Flow

3. admin opens reports and picks a date range. admin sees total billed, total collected, total outstanding, and a month by month chart. admin sees a breakdown by customer and by status. admin can export the current view to CSV for their accountant.

---

# Phase 3 — Polish and compliance

### User Story 1 => Admin Reminder Flow

1. admin turns on payment reminders. the tool emails the customer a polite reminder a set number of days before the due date and again when an invoice becomes overdue, on a schedule the admin controls. each reminder is recorded on the invoice so the team can see what was sent.

---

### User Story 2 => Admin Audit Flow

2. admin opens the activity log and sees every important action, who did it, and when, covering invoice creation, finalization, payment recording, voids, and deletions. deleted records are recoverable from a trash view for a set period before they are purged.

---

# Nice to have

- Multiple currencies per business — pick currency per customer.
- Partial online payment collection — add a pay online button to emailed invoices.
- Customer portal — customers log in to see their own invoices and download PDFs.
- Invoice templates — save a set of line items to reuse without a full recurring plan.
- Tax reports — group tax collected by period for filing.
- Multi-tenant — turn the tool into a SaaS where many businesses sign up.
